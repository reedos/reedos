I work on high-speed and RF hardware for datacenters and photograph wildlife on
weekends. I write software for both, and more recently for working with
language models. These are my public projects.

**[EE Labs](https://github.com/reedos/ee-labs)**: interactive labs for learning
circuits, signals and control, [live here](https://reedos.github.io/ee-labs/).
There are four labs, covering circuit elements and Kirchhoff's laws, filters,
DSP and feedback control. I built them because I enjoy the math behind these
systems. The labs share their underlying code, so a filter built in Circuit Lab
can be opened in Signal Lab with the same resonance and Q. Every claim in a
lab's notes is checked by a test.

**[RF Lab Reference](https://github.com/reedos/rf_lab_reference)**: calculators
for RF bench work, designed for a phone or tablet next to a VNA,
[live here](https://reedos.github.io/rf_lab_reference/). They cover power and
voltage conversion, impedance matching on a Smith chart, return loss and VSWR,
mixed-mode S-parameters, large-signal behavior, sweep planning, cascaded power
and noise, and electrical delay. Each calculator shows the equations it used,
and the settings are saved in the page link.

**[Gradient Ascent](https://github.com/reedos/gradient_ascent)**: a guide to
working with language models,
[live here](https://reedos.github.io/gradient_ascent/). It covers 49 techniques
in eight levels, ordered by how much the model decides on its own, from
answering a single question to running as an independent agent. Each page
explains how the technique works, what it costs and when a simpler one is
enough, and includes a runnable example. The site is still in development: the
pages are based on primary sources, and results from real model runs have not
been added yet.

**[Stack Ledger](https://github.com/reedos/stack_ledger)**: a public research
ledger of the AI buildout, [live here](https://reedos.github.io/stack_ledger/).
It tracks five layers (energy, chips, infrastructure, models and applications)
and separates what has been built and delivered from what has only been
announced. Each event and observation has an evidence grade based on its
source. A model running
locally on my PC researches updates daily, and they are published only after
passing validation and an evidence review.

**[Field Catalog](https://github.com/reedos/field-catalog)**: a Windows desktop
app for culling and cataloging wildlife photos. You can compare the frames of a
burst side by side, mark keepers and rejects, identify species with a local
vision model or an API key, keep a life list and see your photos on a map. It
runs locally and uploads nothing unless you choose to.
